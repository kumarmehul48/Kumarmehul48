Why it feels so hard: running a practice in the USA today

You're not imagining it. The system is genuinely stacked against small and solo practices. Here's what you're up against:

1

Payers deny as a business strategy

Insurers know most practices lack the bandwidth to appeal. A percentage of legitimate claims gets denied on purpose — and every payer has a timely-filing limit (often just 90–180 days). One slow month and that revenue is legally erased. Forever.

2

Prior authorization is a maze built to slow you

Practices spend ~12 hours a week on PA requests. 93% of physicians say it delays care — and it's the top reason claims die before submission. Payers profit from the friction; you absorb the cost.

3

The staffing math doesn't work anymore

A single in-house biller costs $72k–$140k all-in. Billing turnover runs ~30% a year. You can't hire your way out — and every resignation resets your revenue cycle to zero.

4

Reimbursement keeps shrinking

Medicare cut physician payments another 2.83% in 2025 — while rent, wages and supplies climb. The margin that used to absorb billing leakage is gone.

5

Patients owe more than ever

High-deductible plans shifted collections to your front desk. If patient balances aren't estimated, collected and followed up properly, you're financing America's healthcare out of your own pocket.

6

Admin eats your clinical hours

For every hour with a patient, US physicians spend nearly two on paperwork and admin — much of it after hours, at home. This is the burnout you feel but never quite name.

None of this is your failure. It's a system built for hospitals, drop-shipped onto small practices. It runs on one question: who owns your revenue?

The five leaks under your table

Practices don't lose money in one dramatic event. It disappears in small, invisible, ongoing leaks — the ones nobody is paid to watch.

LEAK 01

Denials quietly written off

Up to 90% of denials are recoverable — but most practices never resubmit them. The write-off becomes "normal" and the money is gone.

Invisible loss: recoverable revenue abandoned

LEAK 02

Underpayments never checked

Payers routinely pay less than your contracted rates. If nobody compares payments against contracts, you're financing the difference — forever.

Invisible loss: contracted money never claimed

LEAK 03

A/R aging nobody works

Best practice: under 20% of A/R past 90 days. Most stressed practices sit far above it. After 90 days, collectability collapses — every unworked day shrinks your money.

Invisible loss: earned money aging into nothing

LEAK 04

Eligibility & credentialing gaps

Claims denied before submission even happens — lapsed eligibility checks, outdated payer enrollment, expired credentials. Pure preventable loss.

Invisible loss: claims dead on arrival

LEAK 05 — THE STRUCTURAL ONE

Your revenue process lives in one person's head

With ~30% annual billing turnover, your revenue cycle restarts from zero every time your biller leaves. No documentation, no system, no owner — just chaos that repeats. This is why it never gets fixed: it's nobody's job until it's everybody's crisis.

Invisible loss: every transition resets your revenue
